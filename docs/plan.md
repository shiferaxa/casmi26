# CASMI 2026 plan

Competition: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra

## Facts (verified from public repos on Sep 17 2026, confirm on the Kaggle Rules and Data tabs)

- Task: for each test molecule_id, submit up to 25 SMILES, best first, joined by `;`.
- Metric: MRR@25. A candidate matches if its RDKit tautomer-canonical InChIKey14 equals the truth. Stereo ignored. Implemented in `src/casmi/metric.py`.
- Train: ~2.54M spectra, ~275k distinct structures. Test: 1,213 spectra over 400 molecules, all timsTOF (Bruker). Several spectra per molecule (1 to 16).
- Test has three classes: (1) library knowns, (2) known structure but new spectrum, (3) structures not in any library.
- Public LB is ~33% of test. 5 submissions a day, 2 final picks.
- Dates: entry and team merge Dec 7 2026, final Dec 14 2026 23:59 UTC.
- Prizes: $50k total, $16k for first.
- Code competition: notebook runs with internet off, 9 hour limit, must write `/kaggle/working/submission.csv`. External data (GNPS, COCONUT, RIKEN, PubChem subsets) is being used by public notebooks as attached Kaggle datasets. Confirm on the Rules tab that public external data is allowed before depending on it.

## Data schema (confirmed Sep 17 2026 from test.parquet)

Columns: molecule_id, spectrum_id, ms2_mzs (list), ms2_normalized_intensities (list, base peak = 1), base_peak_intensity, adduct, ionization_mode, instrument_type, precursor_mz, collision_energy_orig, collision_energy_ev (list), collision_energy_orig_units. Train presumably adds smiles. `schema.py` maps these to canonical names.

Test set facts:
- 1,213 spectra, 400 molecules, 1 to 9 spectra per molecule (median 3). All timsTOF, all eV.
- Adducts: [M+H]+ 959, [M-H]- 193, [M+CH2O2-H]- 31, [M+Na]+ 22, [M+NH4]+ 4, [M+K]+ 2, [M+Cl]- 2.
- 108 of 400 molecules have spectra under more than one adduct, often both polarities. Fusing across adducts by structure key is a real advantage: a candidate must explain the neutral mass under every adduct seen.
- Collision energies 20, 40, 60 eV and a merged 20,40,60 spectrum. Negative mode uses -20, -40, -60.
- Precursor m/z 245 to 460, median 329. Mid-size natural products. Candidate databases can be pre-filtered to this neutral mass range (roughly 220 to 470 Da), which shrinks them a lot.
- 4 to 3,259 peaks per spectrum, median 230. Noise filtering matters.

## What the public repos report

A plain library search scores around 0.44 on a naive random holdout but only 0.063 on the public LB. The gap is the timsTOF domain shift plus class 3 molecules. Public notebooks put the ceiling for library-only approaches near 0.15 to 0.23. Anything above that needs candidate retrieval from structure databases plus a learned ranker.

## Competitive landscape (Sep 17 2026)

The public LB cluster at 0.335 to 0.347 (top 50 teams) is one pipeline. Origin is user prvsiyan ("Analog Propagation - CASMI 2026 baseline"), repackaged by haideptry as "0.339 Top 1 4-Channel Transformer Analog Ensemble" (pulled to `artifacts/public/haideptry/`). What it does:

- Candidate pool: COCONUT 2.0 (462k structures) plus all 275k training structures, 711k total, with precomputed packed fingerprints (Morgan r2 and r3 at 4096 bits, RDKit path FP 2048, MACCS, pruned to 6,930 informative bits via `fp_bits.npy`). Dataset prvsiyan/coconut-casmi26-candidates.
- Neutral mass from adduct table (30 adducts, electron mass included). Candidate window is 8.5 ppm around the median neutral mass of all spectra of the molecule, fallback 30 ppm. Window pruned to 80 candidates by library similarity times 100 minus mass difference.
- Channel 1, library: spectral entropy similarity (Li 2021, entropy weighting for low-entropy spectra, floor 0.002, top 256 peaks, 0.01 Da tolerance) against training spectra in the same neutral-mass window, max over library spectra per structure.
- Channel 2, analog: one representative spectrum per training structure (most peaks), window of 200 Da, entropy similarity with the candidate spectrum shifted by the mass difference, keep top 100 analogs. Candidate feature is Tanimoto(candidate FP, analog FP) weighted by analog similarity to the fourth power.
- Channel 3, MetFrag-lite: enumerate 1 and 2 bond breaks of each candidate, score the intensity fraction of peaks explained within 0.01 Da allowing plus or minus 2 hydrogens.
- Channel 4, FPNet: 6 layer, d=512 transformer over up to 128 peak tokens (sinusoidal m/z and neutral-loss embeddings, intensity), plus adduct, instrument family, collision energy, polarity, precursor. Predicts 6,930 fingerprint bit logits. Candidate score is fingerprint dot logits. Two checkpoints (single spectrum and merged-peaks variants, 144 MB each) in prvsiyan/casmi26-fp-models-v2. Training code is not published.
- Ranker: HistGradientBoostingClassifier on 31 per-candidate features (library sim and rank, analog features, model dot product z-scores and ranks, fragmentation features, agreement features between channels), trained on `rank_train.npz` (prvsiyan/casmi26-ranker-features, built off Kaggle, method not published). Ensemble of 2 class-weight priors times 4 seeds. Runtime about 35 min on two T4s.

Reported ablations in its config comments: analog power p=1 gives 0.498, p=4 gives 0.525 on their class 2 validation. ppm 10 vs 8.5 is 0.521 vs 0.524. Their validation is around 0.52 while LB is 0.34, so roughly a third of the test is class 3 or otherwise unreachable for them.

Findings from two more public notebooks (denpugovkin "Four-channel retrieval", LB about 0.332, and wguesdon "Library Plus Fingerprint Ranking", LB 0.248, both in `artifacts/public/`):
- Validation set that matters: the 1,184 timsTOF spectra tagged ingest_lib = enveda-np-examples cover 250 structures the host measured on the test instrument. wguesdon uses them as queries with two masks (library visible, own spectra hidden). Their numbers: library only 0.934 class 1 / 0.000 class 2 / LB 0.146; adding a fingerprint MLP 0.839 / 0.521 / LB 0.227; adding analog propagation 0.835 / 0.570 / LB 0.248. Local gains shrink about half on LB. Add an np-examples split to `validation.py` and report it alongside val2x.
- The fingerprint model is worth about +0.08 LB on its own, the largest single component in every public pipeline. Bernoulli log-likelihood scoring (bits dot logit(p) plus sum log(1-p)) is more principled than the top notebook's raw dot product, which favours candidates with many bits set.
- Bugs in the 0.33 pipeline: the ranker is trained on features from an older pipeline version and inference features silently get truncated to match (X[:, :NFEAT]); the fragmentation channel returns only the molecular mass for anything over 34 bonds, which is most of the test range, so channel 3 is dead weight; the library channel ignores polarity so negative-mode references can match positive queries via the shift trick; the merged model hard-codes collision energy 25.
- Free score the weaker notebook leaves on the table: no fill to 25 candidates, empty windows submit "C", and the learned ranker demotes exact library hits in about one in ten class 1 cases (0.934 to 0.835). A hard rule that a strong library hit goes first fixes that.
- Pool construction in the 0.33 pipeline: COCONUT, then ChEBI and LIPID MAPS (denpugovkin turns these on, haideptry off), then training structures not already present; more than 400 candidates are pruned to library hits plus nearest mass.

Where it can be beaten (in order of expected gain):
1. The fingerprint model. Everyone in the cluster shares the same two checkpoints. A better spectrum-to-fingerprint model is the single largest lever and nobody on the public board has one. Train on all 2.5M spectra with molecule-grouped splits, timsTOF-aware, longer, with a bigger head, and ensemble several. This is Phase 2 and it is now the priority, not Phase 1.
2. Candidate recall. The 80-candidate prune keys on library similarity, which is zero for class 3 molecules, so the true structure gets dropped whenever it is not the closest mass. Prune by model score instead, or keep the full window. Also expand the pool: LOTUS, NPAtlas, and a PubChem slice in the 220 to 470 Da range. Dilution is only a problem if the ranker is weak, which is why item 1 comes first.
3. Ranker trained with a validation that mirrors the test (val2x plus val3 mix), not their unpublished features. A LambdaRank objective on MRR rather than pointwise classification.
4. Multi-adduct consistency: 108 test molecules have spectra under several adducts. A candidate must explain the neutral mass under every adduct. Their median-neutral-mass trick already captures most of this but a hard constraint is cleaner.
5. Public LB is 133 molecules, noise about 0.006. Do not chase it. Pick final submissions by local validation.

Cheap first step to join the cluster: fork the pipeline as-is with the public datasets attached, verify 0.339 reproduces, then swap channel 4 for our own model. That gives a known-good harness to measure every change against.

## Strategy, in order of expected points per hour

### Phase 0, this week: get on the board
1. Join, accept rules, download data, run `scripts/inspect_data.py`, fix `schema.py` aliases.
2. Run `scripts/run_baseline.py --mode val --limit 300000` to confirm the pipeline, then full `--mode submit`.
3. Upload as a Kaggle notebook (see `kaggle/notebook.py`) and submit. Record the LB score in this file.
4. Read the Rules tab and every discussion thread. Note which external datasets top public notebooks attach.

### Phase 1, week 2: reproduce the public 0.339 pipeline as the harness (reordered Sep 17, see landscape above)
- Fork the haideptry kernel with its six datasets attached, run it, confirm 0.339. Copy its adduct table, entropy similarity, and candidate pool loader into `src/casmi`.
- Replace its unpublished ranker training with our own built from val2x and val3 splits.

### Phase 1b, as time allows: library search improvements (classes 1 and 2)
- Validation that matches the test: hold out spectra whose instrument column looks like timsTOF or Bruker QTOF. If there is no instrument column, use collision energy and peak count distributions to find test-like spectra.
- Add modified cosine (shift fragments by precursor mass difference) and spectral entropy similarity, fuse with RRF. Tune ppm window, intensity floor, top N peaks, and bin width on the pseudo split.
- Handle adducts explicitly: the same structure appears as [M+H]+, [M+Na]+, [M+NH4]+ and [M-H]-. Compute neutral mass from precursor and adduct and search on neutral mass, not precursor m/z.
- Merge external spectral libraries (GNPS, MassBank, RIKEN, Enveda's own public sets) into the library if the rules allow. More reference spectra per structure is the single cheapest gain for class 2.
- Expected: 0.15 to 0.25 on public LB.

### Phase 2, weeks 3 to 7: candidate retrieval plus learned ranking (class 2 and 3)
This is where the money is. Class 3 molecules cannot be found by spectrum matching, but their structures usually exist in COCONUT, LOTUS, or PubChem.
- Build a candidate database: COCONUT plus LOTUS (natural products, the test is biological extracts), plus a PubChem slice filtered to natural-product-like mass range. Index by exact mass and formula. Store as a Kaggle dataset so the notebook can use it offline.
- Train a spectrum encoder that predicts a molecular fingerprint (Morgan radius 2, 2048 bits, plus MACCS) from the binned spectrum. Start with an MLP on 1 Da bins, then a transformer over peaks (the MIST paper design). Train on the 2.5M spectra on Kaggle GPU (30 free hours a week, T4 or P100). Loss: binary cross entropy per bit, weighted by bit frequency.
- Rank candidates within the mass window by Tanimoto between predicted and actual fingerprint. This is the CSI:FingerID recipe and is still the strongest published approach for exact structure ID.
- Train a contrastive spectrum-to-structure model (spectrum encoder against a GNN or ECFP encoder of the structure, InfoNCE loss). Use its similarity as a second ranking signal.
- Build a learning-to-rank model (LightGBM lambdarank) over per-candidate features: library cosine, entropy similarity, predicted fingerprint Tanimoto, contrastive score, mass error ppm, formula match, number of spectra agreeing, candidate frequency in the library. Train on the pseudo splits.
- Expected: 0.30+ if the fingerprint model works, based on the CASMI 2022 and MassSpecGym results.

### Phase 3, weeks 7 to 11: refinements
- Formula prediction first (SIRIUS style), then filter candidates by formula. Reduces the candidate pool 10 to 100x.
- Ensembles of several fingerprint models with different seeds and inputs.
- De novo generation (DiffMS or a SMILES transformer conditioned on the spectrum) only as a filler for slots 15 to 25 on molecules where retrieval confidence is low. Exact hits from de novo are rare, so keep it last.
- Pseudo labels: run the final pipeline on the test set, take high-confidence hits, add them to the library.

### Phase 4, final two weeks
- Freeze the pipeline, verify the notebook runs offline within the time limit with a margin.
- Pick the two final submissions: the best public LB run and the best pseudo-validation run. They will differ. Do not pick two runs that only differ on the public LB.
